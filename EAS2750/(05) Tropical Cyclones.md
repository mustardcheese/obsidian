
## Categorization

| Requirement                                      | Type                 |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Organized thunderstorms<br>No closed circulation | Tropical Disturbance |
| Wind <34 kts (39mph)                             | Tropical Depression  |
| 34kts $\leq$ wind $<$ 64kts (74mph)              | Tropical Storm       |
| 64kts $\leq$ wind                                | Hurricane            |
Above the equator, tropical cyclones are called hurricanes (US), and typhoons (Asia)
Below the equator, they are called cyclones

Within 5° of the equator, hurricanes do not form

## Formation

**Warm Sea Surface Temperatures**
- Magic number 26.5°C or 80°F

![[Pasted image 20260914104023.png|400]]



**Sufficiently Deep Layer of Warm Water**
- 60m/200ft of minimum depth
- The convection of the sea water has the ability to strengthen or weaken the storm
![[Pasted image 20260914104111.png|500]]


**Weak Vertical Wind Shear**
- Vertical wind shear is the change in wind speed and/or direction with height
- Strong vertical wind shear tilts the structure and stops weakening
- Tornados need high wind shear because it causes more rotation; Cyclones get their rotation from the Coriolis Effect and wind shear can detract from wind strength

![[Pasted image 20260916103301.png|500]]
	East african coast has very low wind shear

**Sufficient Moisture**
Trivially, mosture is needed but moisture is needed throughout the entire air column, top to bottom
![[Pasted image 20260916103438.png|500]]
	Similarly to wind shear, east african coast is favorable

**Sufficient Coriolis Force**
(5 degrees latitude or greater in either hemisphere)

There requires a certain amount of rotation caused by the rotation of the earth for hurricanes to form. Too close to the equator the coriolis effect doesn't produce enough force
