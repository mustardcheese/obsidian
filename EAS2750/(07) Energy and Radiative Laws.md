
**Energy**: The ability for something to do work
- Kinetic, potential, nuclear, chemical, thermal, radiant, heat

**Conduction**: Heat transfer between molecules or objects in contact
- Holding a hot pan, snow falling on warm ground

**Convection**: heat transfer via motion of a mass of fluid
- Water heating in a pot, *vertical* motion (warm air rising)

**Advection**: transfer of the heat energy by the flow of a fluid, especially horizontally
- Incoming cold front

**Radiation**: energy transferred in the form of electromagnetic waves
- Cooling during clear sky night by radiative cooling

**Electromagnetic Radiation**: energy that travels through space
- The sun is Earth's primary source of energy


### Wavelength

Solar Radiation (Shortwave) -> Visible, UV
Terrestrial Radiation (Longwave) -> Infrared
Based on the wavelength of the energy waves themselves

frequency is inversely proportional to wavelength
$c = \lambda \nu$

### Radiation Principles

**Blackbody Radiation**
An object that is a perfect absorber and emitter of radiation at any given wavelength and temperature
	we generally assume the sun and earth are black bodies, but irl they are greybodies (less efficient than 100%)

When we assume an object is a blackbody the following laws can be applied:
	**Stefan-Boltzman Law**: the total energy radiated by an object is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature ($T^4$)
Warmer objects radiate far more total energy per  unit area than colder objects

$F = \sigma T^4$
* F = Irradiance of "flux" ($\frac{W}{m^{-2}}$ or $\frac{J}{s}$)
* $\sigma$ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant = $5.67\cdot10^{-8}$ ($\frac{W}{m^{-2}} K^{-4}$)
* T = temperature (K)


**Stefan-Boltzmann Law for Graybody**
$F = \epsilon \sigma T^4$

epsilon = emissivity factor (0,1)

The sun primarily emits within UV, Visible, IR (Shortwave)
The earth emits within the IR spectrum (Longwave)

**Wien's Displacement Law**
The wavelength of the most intense radiation is inversely proportional to the temperature of the object

$\lambda_{max} = \frac{2898 \mu m K}{T}$
	$\lambda_{max}$: wavelength of peak emissions $\mu m$
* T = temperature of the object emitting radiation (K)

The warmer the radiating body, the shorter the wavelength of maximum radiation


We calculate the incoming solar radiation recieved from the sun by the inverse square law:
$I_2 = I_1 (\frac{R_1}{R_2})^2$
	$I_1$: intensity of emitted radiation from object 1 with radius $R_1$
	$I_2$: intensity of emitted radiation from object 2 with radius $R_2$
We determine $I_n$ using the Stefan-Boltzmann Law

We can also calculate the terrestrial (LW) radiation emitted by the Earth's surface with the same formula.

**What happens when SW radiation from the sun reaches the Earth?**
1. Reaches Earth's surface (**transmission**)
2. Is **absorbed** within the atmosphere or by an object
3. Is **reflected** or **scattered** off an object and reflected back in space
4. Is reflected or scattered off an object and reaches surface (**diffuse radiation**)

Longwave radiation will:
- Propegate to space
- Be absorbed and re-emitted by the atmosphere
- Be absorbed by greenhouse gasses
- Be absorbed by clouds




### Albedo
Percentage of insolation reflected by an object

Higher albedo -> higher percentage of reflection
Lighter surfaces -> higher albedo, **vice versa**


### Scattering

**Diffuse Reflection**
Objects that scatter radiation in the atmosphere:
- Gas molecules
- Particulates
- Small droplets
- Ice particles
Forward Scattering -> Scattered **away from** the direction of the light
Back Scattering -> Scattered **back towards** the direction of the light


**Rayleigh Scattering**
Scattering by objects ~1/10th the size of incoming wavelengths of radiation
	Probability is approximately ~$\lambda^{-4}$  
Reason for blue skies during the day and red skies at sunrise/sunset

**Mie Scattering**
Scattering by aerosols (larger than air molecules)
- Ex. pollution, salt from sea spray, dust, etc.
Biased towards forward scattering

**Refraction**
Incoming electromagnetic radiation is deflected as it travels from one medium to the next
- Cause of rainbows
- Back scattering phenomena
Rainbows must be back scattered from the sun, and with enough raindrops the white light gets scattered and refracted within raindrops.
