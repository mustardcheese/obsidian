### Water Characteristics
- Water can exist in three phases on Earth: solid liquid and gas
- Has a high heat capacity and specific heat
- Specific heat: amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of 1kg of a substance 1C or 1K

Water vapor concentration is increased via
1. Evaporation (liquid to vapor, in oceans)
2. Sublimation (solid to vapor, via snow/ice)
3. Transpiration (liquid to vapor via vegetation)

When water changes phase, it can result in an absorption or release of latent heat
- Latent heat is the heat used to change phase
- Ice -> Liquid -> Vapor requires external energy (cools the environment) **evaporative cooling**
- Vapor -> Liquid -> Ice releases external energy (warms the environment) **condensation warming**

### Water Phase and Heat Exchange
Latent heat of vaporization $L_v$: heat absorbed (released) from (to) the environment due to evaporation (condensation) per unit mass of water

Latent heat of fusion $L_f$: heat absorbed (released) from (to) the environment due to melting (freezing) per unit mass of water

$L_v = 2.5\times10^6 J kg^{-1}$
$L_f = 3.34\times10^5 J kg^{-1}$


Specific heat is not the same as latent heat. It is how much energy to change the phase **without changing the phase**.

Sublimation vs Deposition
	Sublimation (Deposition): ice (vapor) goes directly to vapor (ice)
Snow cover disappears without melting -- sublimation
Frost on windows -- deposition

# Saturation

## Humidity
Measure of how much water vapor is in the atmosphere

Three types of humidity:
- Absolute humidity
- Specific humidity
- **Relative humidity**
Also dew point temperature and wet bulb temperature

### Absolute Humidity
Density of water vapor in the atmosphere
$\rho_v = \frac{m_{vapor}}{V}$
In reality, absolute humidity isn't really used because it's very difficult to track and measure. It's very hard to get both the air mass and the volume of the atmosphere

### Specific Humidity
$q = \frac{m_{vapor}}{m_{total air}}$
Similarly to absolute humidity, it considers water vapor against total air. However, instead of calculating the density it's by total mass.

Specific humidity increases at the equator and decreases at the poles; specific humidity is tied to the temperature

### Mixing Ratio and Saturation Mixing Ratio
**Mixing Ratio** (w) is the measure of water vapor in air to dry air
$w = \frac{m_{vapor}}{m_{dry}}$

**Saturation Mixing Ratio** ($w_s$) the maximum mixing ratio given the pressure and temperature of the air
$w_s = w_s(T,p)$

### Vapor Pressure
Pressure exerted in air only by water vapor molecules
$p_{air} = p_{dry\ air} + e$
$e << p_{dry\ air}$

Ideal gas law for water vapor $e = \rho_v R_v T$
$R_v = 461.5J kg^{-1}K^{-1}$


### Relative Humidity
Amount of water vapor in air relative to the maximum amount that can be present given the temperature of the air

$RH = \frac{e}{e_s} = \frac{w}{w_s}$ (0 < RH < 1], dimensionless

**Saturation Vapor Pressure**: Upper limit of what e can be
$e_s = e_s(T) = e_0 * e^{\frac{L}{R_v}(\frac{1}{T_0}-\frac{1}{T})}$, Clausius-Clapeyron relationship
	$e_0 = 611.3 Pa$
	$R_v = 461.5 J kg^{-1} K^{-1}$
	$T_0 = 273.15 K$
	$L = L_v = 2.50 \times 10^6 J kg^{-1}$
