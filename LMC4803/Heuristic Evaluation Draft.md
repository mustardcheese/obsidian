**Briefly describe your method**: 
I have used canvas extensively, for my entire college career up to this point as a student, and as of recently as a teaching assistant. To analyze the heuristics I simply looked and used the product myself, interacting with all the buttons, interfaces, files, etc.

**Clearly categorize and present your findings**:
1. Visibility of system status: **Minor Challenge**
Canvas does not do a particularly great job of making system status visible. Whenever you load a new page or tab (assignments, quizzes, etc.) the page will display a loading symbol indicating that something is happening. This is good, however it's implemented inconsistently. In something like the file system:

It will show this loading circle to indicate progress, however when loading an entire page it would hang white instead. This is a minor challenge because there is some implementation of system status, just not consistently throughout the website.


2. Match Between the System and the Real World: **Success**
Canvas does a good job of this by minimizing jargon and allowing for usability. Words on the sidebar such as "Assignments", "Discussions", "Files", "Grades" make it very obvious what it is meant to portray without usage of jargon. Any student, teacher, or parent could understand what these tabs are meant to do and navigate them accordingly. Like a real classroom, the purpose of an assignment, discussion, files, and grades remain the same and understandable.

3. User Control and Freedom: **Serious Challenge**
Canvas does not take lightly to making mistakes on the platform. If a user deletes an assignment by accident or removes all the grades, there is no easy way to revert that action. I have done this before once as a TA where I accidentally deleted a canvas assignment and the associated student grades. As a result, I had to call canvas support which was a hassle. Unlike a word editor, there is no "undo" button. The undoing of an action requires customer service and is thus not easily accessible to users.

4. Consistency and Standards: **Success**
Wording is consistent when it comes to Canvas. Similar to the "match between system and real world" point, the wording of how things and actions are described are consistent throughout canvas with each other.

5. Error Prevention: **Success**
Canvas will warn users before creating a serious decision. In the same vain of user control and freedom, they attempt to warn users before a serious irreversible action is done. And although it would be better for the option to not be irreversible in the first place, they have safeguards put in place to prevent the error from happening.

6. Recognition Rather than Recall: **Success**
Canvas is good for recognition in that the layout is both consistent and visually recognizable. From any page, everything will be in the same place unless edited, and from the view of assignments, you always know what icon corresponds to what. For example, a quiz will be a rocket ship, a file will be a paperclip and all are recognizable.

7. Flexibility and Efficiency of Use: **Success**
Canvas provides a host of keybinds to be used when managing modules, files, assignments, and grading. Whether it be easy ones such as ctrl+c and ctrl+v for copy and pasting assignments, or more complicated ones such as the ones for the speedgrader, they provide a way to make grading and managing classes more efficient.

8. Aesthetic and Minimalist Design: **Success**
Canvas is good at making a medium of minimalist aesthetic. Users can generally pick and choose what they want and don't want to see and the aesthetic can be customized (like with Georgia Tech colors) visually and functionally. The key aspect is the customization, where instructors get the option to choose which tabs show up, allowing for minimalistic course designs.

9. Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors: **Serious Challenge**
Similarly to point 3 with user control and freedom, there is no easy way to recover, diagnose, and recognize errors. Whenever they happen whether or not they are the fault of the user, they almost always need to contact customer service to figure it out. There is no built-in functionality to the platform to fix errors, besides warning them to stop them from happening in the first place. Canvas is very preventative rather than reactive.

10. Help and Documentation: **Success**
Canvas' parent company, Instructure have an easy to find and easy to follow guide for troubleshooting issues. Additionally, Instructure has dedicated canvas helplines specific for each school, allowing for accessible help.
